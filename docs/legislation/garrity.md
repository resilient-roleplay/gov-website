# Garrity Act (GA)
### State of San Andreas – State Register of Laws

*Legislated by the Department of Justice (DOJ).*

---

## Introduction and Purpose

The **Garrity Act (GA)** establishes the legal framework governing **compelled statements made by public employees during administrative investigations** within the State of San Andreas.

This Act ensures that government agencies are able to investigate allegations of employee misconduct while protecting the constitutional rights of public employees against self-incrimination in criminal proceedings.

The Act applies exclusively to administrative or internal investigations and shall not replace or supersede criminal investigative procedures.

---

## Constitutional Justification

This Act is enacted under the authority of the **State Constitution of San Andreas**, protecting the constitutional rights of government employees while preserving the State's ability to maintain professional standards and public confidence.

The Act safeguards the **Right Against Self-Incrimination**, **Right to Due Process**, and the integrity of administrative investigations conducted by government agencies.

---

<details open>
<summary><strong>Article I – Purpose and Scope</strong></summary>

The Garrity Act applies to all government employees employed by agencies within the State of San Andreas.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- Department of Justice (DOJ)
- Los Santos Police Department (LSPD)
- San Andreas Medical Services (SAMS)
- Any other State or Government Agency

This Act applies only to administrative investigations relating to employment or professional misconduct.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Article II – Garrity Warning</strong></summary>

Before compelling an employee to answer questions during an administrative investigation, investigators shall provide a Garrity Warning.

The warning shall inform the employee that:

- They are required to answer questions truthfully.
- Failure to answer may result in administrative discipline.
- Their compelled statements may not be used against them during a criminal prosecution.
- False statements remain subject to disciplinary action and criminal prosecution where applicable.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Article III – Compelled Statements</strong></summary>

Where a Garrity Warning has been issued:

- Employees shall answer all lawful questions truthfully.
- Statements obtained under compulsion shall be used solely for administrative purposes.
- Compelled statements shall not be introduced as evidence during criminal proceedings.
- Administrative investigators shall clearly identify Garrity-protected material within investigative records.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Article IV – Administrative Discipline</strong></summary>

An employee who:

- Refuses to answer lawful questions following a Garrity Warning;
- Knowingly provides false information;
- Intentionally obstructs an administrative investigation;

may be subject to:

- Written reprimand;
- Suspension;
- Demotion;
- Termination of employment;
- Additional disciplinary measures authorized by departmental policy.

Administrative discipline under this Act does not prevent separate criminal investigations where independent evidence exists.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Article V – Criminal Investigations</strong></summary>

Nothing within this Act shall:

- Prevent law enforcement from conducting criminal investigations;
- Prevent criminal prosecution based upon independently obtained evidence;
- Grant immunity from criminal liability.

Where investigators intend to question an employee regarding criminal conduct without compelling answers, appropriate constitutional safeguards, including Miranda warnings where applicable, shall be used instead of a Garrity Warning.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Article VI – Confidentiality</strong></summary>

Administrative investigative records containing Garrity-protected statements shall be treated as confidential personnel records.

Such records may only be disclosed:

- Pursuant to a lawful court order;
- As required by law;
- For legitimate administrative purposes.

Unauthorized disclosure may result in disciplinary action.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Article VII – Judicial Interpretation</strong></summary>

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the constitutional protections established in:

**Garrity v. New Jersey, 385 U.S. 493 (1967).**

Where ambiguity exists, courts shall favor interpretations protecting constitutional rights while permitting legitimate administrative oversight.

</details>

---

## Conclusion

The **Garrity Act** ensures that government agencies may investigate employee misconduct effectively while preserving the constitutional protections afforded to public employees.

It balances accountability, transparency, and due process by allowing administrative investigations to proceed without compromising the fairness of future criminal proceedings.
