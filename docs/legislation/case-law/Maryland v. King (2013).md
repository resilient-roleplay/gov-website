# Maryland v. King (2013)

---

## Overview
**Maryland v. King** addressed the legality of **DNA swabbing** during the booking process after a lawful arrest.  
Officers took a DNA sample from the defendant, which linked him to an unsolved rape case.


## Ruling
The Supreme Court held that **DNA collection during booking** is a **reasonable search** under the Fourth Amendment. It serves legitimate state interests in identifying arrestees and solving prior crimes.

## Key Holding
> “When officers make an arrest supported by probable cause, taking and analyzing a cheek swab of the arrestee’s DNA is, like fingerprinting and photographing, a legitimate police booking procedure.”

## Implication
This decision confirmed DNA swabbing as a **standard, constitutional identification method** during booking, similar to fingerprinting or photographing.

## Quick Guide
- DNA collection is lawful upon **booking for serious offenses**.
- Can be used to link suspects to **unsolved or ongoing investigations**.
- Officers must ensure samples are **handled and logged properly**.
- Does **not violate privacy rights** when done post-arrest under proper procedure.
