# Miranda v. Arizona (1966)

---

## Overview
**Miranda v. Arizona** established that suspects must be **informed of their rights** before custodial interrogation.  
The defendant confessed without being told of his right to remain silent or have legal counsel present.


## Ruling
The Supreme Court held that statements made during custodial interrogation are **inadmissible** unless the suspect has been **informed of and waived** their constitutional rights.


## Key Holding
> “The prosecution may not use statements stemming from custodial interrogation unless it demonstrates the use of procedural safeguards effective to secure the privilege against self-incrimination.”



## Implication
The **Miranda Warning** became mandatory when conducting custodial interrogation.  
Failure to administer the warning invalidates any statement obtained.



## Quick Guide
- Read **Miranda Rights** before questioning any suspect in custody.
- Not required until **interrogation** is about to occur.
- Delay rights reading if suspect requires **medical care or sobriety** first.
- Once rights are invoked, all questioning must **immediately cease** until counsel is present.
